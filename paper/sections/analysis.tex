\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

We organize the analysis around four mechanism questions.

\paragraph{Q1: Where do the gains live---prompts or weights?}
Two observations point the same way. The first is the self-segregation
result of \S\ref{sec:exp-fused}: cross-domain transfer occurs while prompts
remain domain-pure, so the signal must travel through the weights. The second
is the source-mastery evaluation, which strips all scaffolding (no
demonstrations, no memory): the trained adapter alone retains a $+16.7$-point
gain over base ($.480$ vs.\ $.313$). ICL's gains, by construction, do not
survive this test.

\takeaway{The improvement survives removal of all prompting scaffolding and crosses domain boundaries that retrieval never crosses: it lives in the weights.}

\paragraph{Q2: What does forward re-evaluation buy?}
Re-evaluation converts weight improvements back into training data. In the fused
run, 84 of 1{,}463 memory entries (${\sim}6\%$) came from problems \emph{failed}
on arrival and recovered later by the improved model---examples a frozen-model
method could never harvest, since re-answering with an unchanged model
reproduces the same failure. Recovery concentrates early (most recoverable
problems are caught within their window), after which re-evaluation's remaining
role is refreshing stored answers into the current model's phrasing, keeping
distillation targets near on-policy. Updates improve the model, the improved model recovers problems it
previously failed, and the recovered problems become training data.

\paragraph{Q3: Why is self-distillation stable where RL is not?}
The collapse signature of REINFORCE++ on the fused stream (negative approximate
KL, clip-fraction spike, exploding advantage variance) is characteristic of
policy-gradient divergence: the objective chases a moving, high-variance
advantage estimate, an instability documented for RLVR at scale---entropy
collapse is intrinsic to the policy gradient \citep{cui2025entropy}, and even
successful long-horizon RLVR requires explicit KL control and periodic
reference-policy resets \citep{liu2025prorl}. The distillation objective (Eq.~\ref{eq:kl}) has none of
these moving parts. Its target distribution comes from a frozen teacher, its targets are
oracle-verified text, and the disagreement between student and teacher is
bounded by the information in the hint. Empirically, per-batch KL
loss stays in $[10^{-3}, 10^{-1}]$ across all runs, and no run among
${\sim}12{,}000$ streamed problems ever regressed catastrophically. Our frozen-base teacher acts as an always-on
reference anchor---the safeguard ProRL adds by periodic resets, built into the
objective. This echoes, in the online setting, the offline finding that
self-generated targets minimize distributional drift
\citep{yang2024selfdistillation}: our stored answers are
by construction near the model's own distribution, so each update is a small,
verified step.

\takeaway{Stability comes from the objective, not from tuning: a frozen teacher and verified targets remove every moving part that destabilizes policy gradients.}

\paragraph{Q4: Why does the online schedule beat batch epochs on identical data?}
Three ingredients of the stream are absent in batch training. \emph{Curriculum}:
a problem enters training only when the model (current, not initial) solves it,
so supervision always sits at the model's frontier. \emph{Repetition with
change}: a problem is revisited ${\sim}W$ times across its window while the
model evolves, each pass distilling against a slightly different student.
\emph{Late harvest}: re-evaluation adds problems as they come in reach. Batch
generation with a single greedy sample per epoch can only ever train on what the
current model already solves deterministically; its coverage grew from $38.5\%$
to $41.8\%$ across four epochs while the online run ended with verified answers
for $53.5\%$ of problems. The mastery gap ($.480$ vs.\ $.425$) tracks this coverage gap, and the
exploration-matched arm confirms the account: sampling restores coverage
($56.5\%$), mastery ($.472$), and transfer ($.574$ vs.\ $.567$ on the unseen
stream). Coverage of verified successes, however obtained, is the quantity
that matters; the stream is the mechanism that obtains it in one pass.

\paragraph{Integrity of the streaming metric.}
Because every reported number is produced by a model that had never seen that
problem---scored before storage, demonstrations strictly from the past, hints
strictly self-generated---the prequential metric doubles as a continual
generalization measure. We release a causal-ordering audit that verifies these
invariants over the raw run logs (all checks pass; Appendix~\ref{app:leakage}).
